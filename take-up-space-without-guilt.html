How to Take Up Space Without Feeling Guilty

For a long time, many women learn how to be impressive without being too visible.

Be smart—but not intimidating.

Be ambitious—but not too ambitious.

Speak—but don't dominate.

Look good—but don't seem like you're trying.

Have opinions—but don't make anyone uncomfortable.

Eventually, you become very good at editing yourself.

Taking up space challenges that pattern.

It means allowing yourself to be seen, heard, and fully present without treating your existence like an inconvenience.

Here are 7 ways to practice.

1. Speak Before Your Thought Is Perfect

You don't need the perfect sentence.

You don't need to rehearse every possibility.

You don't need to sound flawless.

Say what you think.

Let yourself participate.

2. Stop Apologizing for Existing

"I'm sorry."

"I'm sorry to bother you."

"Sorry, can I ask something?"

Notice how often you apologize when you haven't actually done anything wrong.

Replace unnecessary apologies with clear statements.

"Thank you for your patience."

"Can I add something?"

"I need clarification."

Your voice doesn't require an apology.

3. Let Your Accomplishments Be Visible

You don't need to brag.

But you also don't need to immediately minimize every accomplishment.

You worked hard.

You achieved something.

You learned something.

Let it be true.

4. Say What You Actually Want

Instead of:

"Whatever works for everyone."

Try:

"I'd prefer…"

"I want…"

"I'm looking for…"

"I'd like…"

Get the Self-Worth Reset starter guide, free.

Clarity is a form of self-respect.

5. Stop Over-Explaining Your Boundaries

A boundary becomes weaker when you believe you need to convince someone to agree with it.

You can explain when explanation is helpful.

But you don't need permission to have a limit.

6. Get Comfortable Being Seen

Visibility can feel uncomfortable when you've spent years trying not to draw attention to yourself.

Start small.

Post your work.

Share your perspective.

Introduce yourself.

Ask the question.

Take the seat.

Let people know what you do.

The goal isn't attention.

It's comfort with your own presence.

7. Remember That You Belong in the Room

You don't have to become the smartest, loudest, or most accomplished person in the room to deserve your seat.

You don't need to earn your right to exist there in real time.

Stand tall.

Listen.

Contribute.

Learn.

Be present.

Your Next Step

Today, identify one place where you've been making yourself smaller.

Then ask:

"What would taking up 10% more space look like?"

Not 100%.

Just 10%.

Take that step.

You just have to stop disappearing.
