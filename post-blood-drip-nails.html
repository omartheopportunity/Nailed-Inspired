Blood Drip Nails: How to Get Clean Drips With a Dotting Tool

Sept 28, 2026 · 4 min read

Blood drip nails have a reputation for being an advanced, salon-only design. They're not. The whole effect comes down to one small tool and doing the steps in the right order — most of what makes a drip look "clean" instead of smudged is patience between layers, not skill.

What you actually need

A glossy black or deep plum base, a small dotting tool (or a toothpick in a pinch), and a red or oxblood polish with some body to it — thin, watery formulas spread instead of holding a drip shape.

The order that keeps drips clean

Start the drip at the cuticle with a small dot, then drag the dotting tool down in one short, controlled pull instead of painting a line. Let that fully dry before adding a second drip layer on top — going back in too soon is what causes the color to bleed and lose its shape.

One drip, fully dry, before the next one. That's the entire technique.

Finishing it

Seal with a glossy top coat once everything is dry, which also deepens the red and gives the whole set a wet-look shine. For a softer take on the trend, keep the drips to one or two accent nails instead of the full set.
