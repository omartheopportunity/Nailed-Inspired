Couples Halloween Costumes That Actually Match Your Nails

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Behavioral researchers Francesca Gino and Michael Norton have studied what they call the "ritual effect" — the finding that small, shared, somewhat arbitrary rituals measurably increase feelings of connection between people, independent of how meaningful the ritual objectively is. Coordinating a Halloween costume with a matching or complementary manicure is exactly this kind of low-stakes shared ritual: it doesn't need to mean anything profound to do real relational work.

Why the detail matters more than the costume

Most couples' costume planning stops at the obvious layer — who's wearing what. Carrying the coordination down to a shared detail, like nails that echo each other's costume colors, extends the ritual past the big visible choice into something smaller and more personal, which is often the part people actually remember afterward.

It works for any pair, not just romantic ones

The ritual research doesn't specify romantic partners — the connection effect shows up between friends, sisters, coworkers, anyone sharing a small coordinated act on purpose. A matched manicure works the same way between best friends as it does between a couple.

Connection isn't built in the big gesture. It's built in the small, coordinated ones you chose to do together.

The fresh reframe

Matching nails to a costume looks like a small styling detail. Functionally, it's a tiny shared ritual — and per the research on what actually strengthens relationships, tiny shared rituals carry more weight than their size would suggest.
