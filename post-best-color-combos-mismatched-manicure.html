Best Color Combos for a Mismatched Manicure

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Psychologist Daniel Berlyne's influential research on aesthetic preference found that people consistently rate moderate complexity as most pleasing — not the most uniform, predictable option, and not the most chaotic one either, but something with enough variety to stay interesting while still holding together. A well-planned mismatched manicure, using two or three related colors across ten nails, sits exactly in that moderate-complexity sweet spot.

Why fully matched can feel flat

Ten identical nails offer very little for the eye to explore — which, per Berlyne's research, tends to read as visually flat rather than calming. A mismatched manicure introduces enough variation to hold interest without tipping into visual chaos, as long as the colors are chosen with some relationship to each other.

The key is coordinated, not random

Berlyne's "moderate" complexity is the operative word — ten completely unrelated colors would overshoot into the kind of high complexity people rate as unpleasant. The strongest mismatched manicures pull from a tight, coordinated palette, which is what keeps the variety in that pleasing middle zone rather than sliding into visual noise.

The most pleasing option isn't the simplest or the busiest one. It's the one with just enough variety to stay interesting.

The fresh reframe

A mismatched manicure isn't a chaotic alternative to a matched one. Done with a coordinated palette, it's actually landing in the exact complexity range research says people find most visually satisfying.
