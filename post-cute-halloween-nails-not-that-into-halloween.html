Cute Halloween Nails for the Girl Who's Not That Into Halloween

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Learning theorists Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger introduced the idea of "legitimate peripheral participation" to describe how people can meaningfully belong to a community or event without fully immersing themselves in it — participation doesn't have to be all-or-nothing to count as real. A small, cute Halloween manicure, worn by someone who has no interest in costumes, candy, or haunted houses, is a clean example of that same idea.

You don't need to love the holiday to participate in it

There's an unspoken pressure that engaging with Halloween at all means going all in — decorations, a full costume, a themed party. Lave and Wenger's research suggests that's not actually how participation works in most communities: a small, genuine gesture is a legitimate form of involvement on its own, not a lesser version of full commitment.

What peripheral participation looks like here

A single cute pumpkin or a soft ghost outline, worn without any other nod to the holiday, is exactly this — a real acknowledgment of the season that doesn't require pretending to be more into it than you are.

Belonging doesn't require full immersion. A small, genuine gesture counts as real participation.

The fresh reframe

You're allowed to be lukewarm on Halloween and still wear something cute for it. That's not halfhearted — it's a legitimate, low-key way of joining in on your own terms.
