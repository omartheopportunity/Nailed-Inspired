This Season, Start With Your Hands — the Rest Follows

Sept 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Psychologist Solomon Asch's classic research on impression formation found what's now known as the primacy effect: information encountered first carries disproportionate weight in shaping the overall impression that follows, more than information that comes later, even when it's objectively no more important. Starting a seasonal shift with your hands — the detail you notice constantly, all day, before anything else changes — is a direct application of that same principle to how you experience your own reinvention.

Why the first change sets the tone for everything after

A bigger shift — a new wardrobe, a new routine, a new chapter — can feel too large to start all at once. Per the primacy effect, you don't need to start with the biggest piece. Whatever comes first ends up coloring how you experience everything that follows it, which means a small, immediate change like a manicure can set a disproportionately positive tone for the rest.

Hands are a genuinely good place to start

Because you see your own hands constantly, all day, a change there functions almost like a running first impression — repeated to yourself, over and over, setting the tone each time you catch sight of them.

What comes first shapes everything that follows more than it should, by rights, be allowed to.

The fresh reframe

Starting with your hands this season isn't starting small. Per what's actually known about how first impressions work, it might be starting exactly where it matters most.
