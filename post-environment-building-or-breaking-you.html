ENVIRONMENT

Your Environment Is Either Building You or Breaking You

Sept 2, 2026 · 7 min read

You can have the biggest goals in the world and still struggle to move forward if your environment constantly pulls you backward.

That's because your environment doesn't just surround you.

It shapes you.

The people you talk to. The conversations you have. The content you consume. The standards you see. The opportunities you're exposed to. The behavior that's considered "normal."

All of it quietly influences what you believe is possible for your life.

And if you're serious about becoming a different version of yourself, you have to start paying attention to the rooms you're building inside.

Your Environment Is More Powerful Than Motivation

We often treat personal growth like a willpower problem.

"I just need to be more disciplined." "I need to stay motivated." "I need more self-control."

But imagine trying to build confidence while constantly surrounded by people who criticize you. Trying to become ambitious while everyone around you tells you to settle. Trying to build healthy habits while your environment constantly rewards unhealthy ones. Trying to become more visible while your social circle makes you feel embarrassed for putting yourself out there.

You might succeed anyway.

But you're making the process unnecessarily difficult.

Your environment can either reinforce the identity you're building — or reinforce the identity you're trying to leave behind.

The People Around You Become Reference Points

We naturally compare our behavior to what's normal around us.

If everyone around you complains about their job, dissatisfaction can start feeling normal.

If everyone avoids taking risks, playing it safe feels normal.

If everyone is building businesses, investing in themselves, networking, or pursuing ambitious goals, those behaviors start feeling normal too.

That's why your circle matters.

Not because you need to surround yourself with "successful" people for status.

Because exposure changes your perception of possibility.

Sometimes you don't need someone to give you the answer.

You need someone to show you that the answer exists.

Your Environment Can Affect Your Standards

Standards are contagious.

Think about the people you spend the most time with. What do they tolerate? What do they celebrate? What do they consider acceptable? What do they expect from themselves? What do they expect from you?

Over time, these things become reference points.

If disrespect is normal around you, healthy boundaries can feel extreme.

If mediocrity is normal, ambition can feel excessive.

If everyone expects little from themselves, expecting more from yourself can feel uncomfortable.

That discomfort doesn't necessarily mean you're wrong.

Sometimes it means you're changing.

Your Digital Environment Counts Too

Your environment isn't only physical.

Your phone is an environment. Your feed is an environment. Your group chats are environments. The creators you consume are part of the environment shaping your thoughts.

Ask yourself: What am I repeatedly exposing my mind to?

Are you constantly consuming comparison? Drama? Fear? Outrage? Perfection?

Or are you consuming ideas that challenge you, educate you, inspire you, and expand your perspective?

You don't need to delete every account that doesn't serve you.

But you should know what you're feeding your mind every day.

The Right Environment Doesn't Mean Constant Praise

A supportive environment isn't one where everyone tells you you're amazing.

Sometimes the best people challenge you. They ask better questions. They point out blind spots. They hold you accountable. They tell you when your plan doesn't make sense.

The difference is that their goal isn't to keep you small.

They're trying to help you grow.

You want people who can challenge your thinking without attacking your identity.

Build Before You Leave

Sometimes people realize their environment isn't helping them and immediately think: "I need new friends."

Maybe.

But don't wait for a completely new life before becoming intentional.

Start building the environment you want now. Attend events. Reach out to people. Join communities. Start conversations. Invite someone to coffee. Share your goals. Create spaces where ambitious conversations can happen.

You don't need to know 100 new people.

You may only need a few relationships that genuinely expand you.

Ask Yourself These Five Questions

Take a moment and answer honestly.

Who makes me feel most like the woman I want to become?

Who consistently pulls me toward an older version of myself?

What conversations dominate my current environment?

What behaviors have become normal because of the people around me?

What kind of room would make my goals feel more normal?

Your answers are data.

Use them.

Your Next Level Needs a New Normal

Growth becomes easier when your environment stops making your goals feel unusual.

You want rooms where discipline is normal. Where ambition isn't embarrassing. Where asking questions is encouraged. Where people introduce you to people. Where success isn't treated like a threat. Where becoming more of yourself is celebrated.

That's ultimately what the right environment provides: a new normal.

You aren't looking for people who have the exact same life as you.

You're looking for people who are moving in a direction that makes you want to move too.

Build a Better Room

Your next chapter isn't just about becoming a better version of yourself.

It's about becoming intentional about the environments that shape that version.
