Halloween Nail Ideas That Double as Your Whole Costume

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Economist and Nobel laureate Herbert Simon coined the term "satisficing" to describe a decision strategy that's often smarter than optimizing: instead of exhaustively searching for the perfect solution, you choose the first option that's good enough to meet your actual needs, and move on. Letting your nails carry the entire costume — instead of assembling a full outfit — is satisficing applied directly to Halloween planning.

Why "good enough" often wins here

A full costume search — shopping, fitting, coordinating accessories — is a classic optimization trap: there's always a slightly better version out there if you keep looking. A striking nail design that reads clearly as "witch" or "vampire" or "ghost" on its own sidesteps that whole search. It's not a lesser costume. It's the satisficing solution that gets you into the spirit of the day without the optimization spiral.

Efficient doesn't mean uncommitted

Simon's research is often misread as an argument for laziness. It's really an argument for matching effort to what a decision actually requires. A costume-level nail design still requires real skill and intention — it just skips the parts of costume planning that weren't adding much value anyway.

The smartest decision isn't always the most exhaustive one. Sometimes it's the one that's good enough and done.

The fresh reframe

Letting your nails do the costume's job isn't cutting corners. It's the same decision logic that separates efficient choices from exhausting ones — applied to Halloween instead of everything else it usually shows up in.
