The Ghost-French Manicure You Didn't Know You Needed

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Nostalgia researcher Constantine Sedikides has spent years studying why nostalgic imagery reliably boosts mood and makes people feel more connected — not just to the past, but to the people around them in the present. A French manicure is one of the most nostalgic shapes in beauty: nearly everyone has a memory attached to it. Twisting that familiar shape into a ghost tip doesn't discard the nostalgia. It borrows it.

Why a remix beats a reinvention

Completely original ideas ask an audience to do more cognitive work — they have to figure out what they're looking at before they can decide if they like it. A familiar shape with one unexpected twist skips that step entirely. You already know what a French tip is. Your eye goes straight to what's different about it, which is exactly where the ghost-tip design wants your attention.

The comfort of the familiar, with something new to say

This is the same principle behind why remakes, revivals, and "the classic but make it modern" content consistently performs well across categories, not just beauty. People aren't looking to be surprised out of nowhere. They're looking for a new way to feel something they already know they like.

The most memorable version of an idea is rarely the newest one. It's the familiar one, told slightly differently.

The fresh reframe

You don't have to invent something no one's ever seen to stand out. Sometimes the more powerful move is taking something everyone already loves and adding one true, unexpected detail. That's not a lack of originality. That's knowing exactly what to keep and what to change.
