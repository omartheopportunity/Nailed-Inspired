Becoming Her

7 Ways to Start Showing Up Like the Woman You Want to Be

There is a version of you that exists in your imagination. At some point, your behavior has to catch up with your vision.

She handles herself differently.

She walks into rooms differently.

She speaks differently.

She has stronger boundaries.

She takes her goals seriously.

She doesn't constantly ask everyone else for permission.

Maybe you've been calling her your "future self."

But here's the problem:

You can't think your way into becoming her.

At some point, your behavior has to catch up with your vision.

Becoming the woman you want to be isn't about pretending to have everything figured out.

It's about beginning to make decisions that align with the identity you're trying to build.

Here are seven ways to start showing up like her now.

1. Make Decisions Based on Who You're Becoming

Before making a decision, ask yourself:

"Does this support the woman I'm becoming?"

That question can change a lot.

Should you spend the next three hours scrolling?

Should you reach out to that person?

Should you accept that invitation?

Should you continue that relationship?

Should you finally start the project?

You don't need to make every decision perfectly.

You just need to become more intentional.

Your future identity becomes clearer when your daily choices start reflecting it.

2. Keep the Promises You Make to Yourself

If you want to trust yourself, become trustworthy to yourself.

This means doing what you said you would do.

Start small.

If you say you're going to wake up at a certain time, try.

If you say you're going to work on your business for 30 minutes, do it.

If you say you're going to stop contacting someone who consistently disrupts your peace, follow through.

Every time you keep a promise, you're reinforcing:

"I do what I say I'm going to do."

That is identity-building.

3. Raise Your Standards

The woman you're becoming doesn't necessarily have more than you.

She may simply tolerate less of what consistently takes her away from herself.

She has standards for:

  • How she is treated
  • How she spends her time
  • Who gets access to her
  • What she consumes
  • What environments she enters
  • How she speaks to herself
  • What goals she pursues

Raising your standards isn't about becoming judgmental.

It's about becoming intentional.

Ask:

"What have I normalized that no longer aligns with who I want to be?"

Then change one thing.

4. Put Yourself in Better Rooms

You cannot underestimate the power of environment.

If you want to become more entrepreneurial, spend time around entrepreneurs.

If you want to become more confident, spend time around people who encourage you to express yourself.

If you want to grow professionally, build relationships with people who are already operating at the level you're pursuing.

You don't need to know exactly how they'll help you.

Sometimes the first benefit of a new environment is simply discovering:

"People like me actually do this."

Your environment can expand your idea of what's possible.

5. Let Yourself Be Seen Before You Feel Ready

The woman you're becoming cannot remain invisible forever.

Eventually you have to:

  • Share the idea
  • Post the content
  • Introduce yourself
  • Apply for the opportunity
  • Ask the question
  • Say what you want
  • Let people know what you're building

Being visible can feel vulnerable.

Especially if you're used to worrying about what people think.

But hiding protects you from rejection while also protecting you from opportunity.

You don't need everyone to approve of you.

You need to become comfortable enough with yourself to let yourself be seen.

6. Take Your Goals Seriously

There's a difference between wanting something and organizing your life around it.

If you say you want to build a business but never give it time, it remains an idea.

If you say you want stronger relationships but never meet new people, it remains a wish.

If you say you want to change your life but your daily schedule leaves no room for change, the intention won't be enough.

Take your goals seriously by giving them:

Time. Attention. Action.

You don't need to dedicate your entire life to them.

But they need a place in your actual calendar.

7. Stop Waiting for Permission

This may be the hardest shift.

At some point, nobody is coming to officially announce:

"You're ready now."

You may still feel inexperienced.

You may still have doubts.

Someone may not understand your decision.

You may not have everyone's approval.

Go anyway.

You don't need permission to want more.

You don't need permission to change.

You don't need permission to outgrow an old identity.

You don't need permission to pursue something meaningful.

The woman you're becoming learns to make decisions from self-respect rather than approval.

Become Her Through Action

You don't become your future self by obsessing over who she is.

You become her by repeatedly behaving in ways that reinforce that identity.

Every time you choose discipline over avoidance, you cast a vote.

Every time you speak up, you cast a vote.

Every time you protect your time, you cast a vote.

Every time you enter a new room, you cast a vote.

Every time you take your goals seriously, you cast a vote.

Eventually, those votes add up.

And one day, what once felt like "acting like her" simply becomes who you are.

Your Next Step

Pick one of these seven shifts.

Don't pick all seven.

Ask yourself:

"Which one would create the biggest difference in the woman I become if I practiced it for the next 30 days?"

Then put it somewhere you can see it every day.
