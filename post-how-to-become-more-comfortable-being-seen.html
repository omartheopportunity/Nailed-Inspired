Becoming Her

How to Become More Comfortable Being Seen

You can be talented and still hide. Learning to be seen isn't about becoming an attention seeker — it's about letting yourself exist fully.

You can have great ideas and never share them.

You can know what you want and still struggle to say it out loud.

You can be ambitious and still feel uncomfortable letting people see how ambitious you actually are.

That's the problem with waiting until you feel confident enough to be visible.

You may wait forever.

Learning how to become more comfortable being seen isn't about becoming an attention seeker.

It's about becoming comfortable allowing yourself to exist fully, your ideas, opinions, goals, personality, ambition, and needs, without constantly editing yourself for other people's approval.

Why Being Seen Can Feel So Uncomfortable

Being visible creates the possibility of judgment.

Someone might disagree.

Someone might criticize you.

Someone might ignore you.

Someone might think you're trying too hard.

Someone might not understand what you're building.

So hiding can feel safer.

But there's a trade-off.

The same visibility that exposes you to judgment also exposes you to:

Opportunity.

Connections.

Clients.

Friendships.

Mentorship.

Collaboration.

Recognition.

Growth.

You can't control whether everyone likes what they see.

But you can control whether you allow yourself to be seen.

Here are seven ways to become more comfortable with it.

1. Start With Low-Stakes Visibility

You don't have to begin by putting your entire life online.

Start small.

Share an opinion with a friend.

Ask a question in a group.

Introduce yourself to someone new.

Comment thoughtfully on someone's post.

Practice speaking up during a meeting.

You're building your tolerance for visibility.

2. Stop Assuming Everyone Is Watching

Most people are far more focused on themselves than they are on you.

The mistake is imagining that everyone is analyzing your every move.

They're not.

And even when someone notices you, they may forget about it five minutes later.

You don't need to perform perfectly because the audience in your head is usually much larger than the audience in reality.

3. Let Yourself Have an Opinion

You don't have to turn every conversation into a debate.

But you should be able to say:

"I think..."

"I prefer..."

"I disagree..."

"I'd like..."

"I don't think that's right for me."

Having an opinion doesn't make you difficult.

It means you're present.

4. Say What You Want

One of the most powerful forms of visibility is simply being honest about what you want.

Want to start a business?

Say it.

Want a different career?

Say it.

Want more meaningful friendships?

Say it.

Want the opportunity?

Ask.

People cannot support a version of you that you've never allowed them to meet.

5. Stop Waiting for Universal Approval

Someone will misunderstand you.

Someone won't like your approach.

Someone may think you're unrealistic.

That's part of being visible.

Your goal isn't universal approval.

Your goal is alignment.

You want the right people to recognize themselves in what you're building.

6. Practice Taking Up Space

Sit at the table.

Speak clearly.

Don't apologize for every sentence.

Don't automatically defer when you have something valuable to contribute.

Don't shrink your accomplishments so other people feel comfortable.

Taking up space doesn't mean dominating the room.

It means accepting that you belong in it too.

7. Remember Why You're Showing Up

Visibility becomes easier when it stops being about proving yourself.

You're not posting because everyone needs to like you.

You're sharing because someone might need what you know.

You're networking because the right relationship could change your trajectory.

You're speaking because your perspective has value.

You're building because the life you want requires participation.

Purpose makes visibility easier to tolerate.

You Don't Need to Become Fearless

The goal isn't to become a woman who never feels exposed.

It's to become a woman who can feel exposed and still participate.

That's confidence.

The woman you're becoming may still feel nervous before she enters the room.

She may still wonder what people think.

She may still have moments of self-doubt.

But she stops allowing those feelings to make every decision.

Your Next Step

Choose one low-risk way to be more visible today.

Speak up.

Introduce yourself.

Share the idea.

Ask for the opportunity.

Tell someone what you're building.

Then notice how you feel afterward.
