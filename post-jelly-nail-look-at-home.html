How to Get the Jelly Nail Look at Home

Sept 27, 2026 · 4 min read

The sheer, glossy "glazed" nail look that swept beauty media in 2022 traces back to Hailey Bieber's own manicure, widely reported at the time as being inspired by a glazed donut — a look built on a translucent, milky polish rather than an expensive salon technique. The jelly nail trend that followed runs on the same principle: sheer, light-reflective polish over a bare or barely-tinted nail, layered thin.

Why this one travels well from salon to bathroom counter

Unlike hand-painted nail art, the jelly effect isn't about steady-hand skill. It's about product choice and thin, patient layering. That makes it one of the rare high-impact trends that genuinely doesn't require a nail tech's precision — just a sheer polish, two or three thin coats, and a good top coat for the shine.

The three things that actually matter

Buff the nail lightly before you start so the polish has something to grip. Apply thin coats — thin is the whole trick, since a thick sheer coat goes streaky instead of glassy. And finish with a high-shine top coat, since the "wet look" effect lives almost entirely in the top layer.

The most photographed manicures aren't always the most technical ones. Sometimes they're just the best-executed simple idea.

The fresh reframe

High-end beauty trends get marketed like they require a high-end budget. This one doesn't. The jelly nail look proves that the gap between a $90 salon visit and a $9 bottle of polish is sometimes just patience — not skill, not money, just three thin coats and the willingness to let each one dry.
