Halloween Nails for the Girl Who's Becoming Someone New This Fall

Sept 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Anthropologist Victor Turner, building on Arnold van Gennep's earlier work on rites of passage, described a "liminal" stage that sits between an old identity and a new one — a real, recognized in-between space in nearly every documented culture's transition rituals. A change of season, especially the shift into fall, functions as a familiar, low-stakes version of that same liminal window: not who you were over summer, not yet fully settled into who you're becoming.

Why a manicure fits the liminal moment so well

Rites-of-passage research notes that liminal stages are often marked by some outward, visible change — a new garment, an altered appearance — that helps make the internal shift feel real. A new manicure, timed to a season change, does that same marking work: something visible and immediate, while the bigger internal shift is still taking shape.

You don't need the whole transformation figured out yet

Turner's framework treats the liminal stage as valuable in its own right, not just a gap to rush through. You're allowed to be in the middle of becoming something without having the full picture — the manicure can mark the shift starting, not the shift completed.

Every culture that studies transition recognizes the in-between stage as real. You don't have to already be the finished version to mark that you're changing.

The fresh reframe

Halloween nails for a girl becoming someone new this fall aren't just seasonal. They're marking a real, well-documented kind of transition — the in-between stage every meaningful change actually passes through.
