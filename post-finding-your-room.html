Finding Your Room: What Real Community Looks Like

Sept 2, 2026 · 4 min read

There's a specific kind of lonely that comes from being ambitious around people who aren't. It's not that they're unkind — it's that your goals don't quite make sense to them, so they respond with caution instead of momentum.

You mention the idea. They ask if you've thought it through. You mention the goal. They ask if it's realistic. None of it is malicious. It's just a different room than the one you actually need.

Two kinds of circles

One circle asks, why are you doing all that? The other says, let's build. Both can love you. Only one of them will help you move.

You don't have to become her alone

The instinct, when you feel out of step with your circle, is to go quiet about your ambition — to shrink the goal so it stops feeling like a gap between you and everyone else. The better move is to go find the room where the goal is normal.

Better rooms don't just support the plan. They change what feels possible in the first place.

You're not asking for permission to want more. You're just looking for the people who already do.
